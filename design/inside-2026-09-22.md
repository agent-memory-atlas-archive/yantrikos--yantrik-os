changed, and I could confirm each one as a user rather than as an author.
**#94** — the grammar in `describe` is not just present, it is sufficient: two non-trivial specs,
first try, 271 ms of typing, no source, no retries. That is the strongest result of the evening.
**#101** — `set_backend` does not crash, and the grade moves where it should: in `describe`, and

**#97** — `frame_budget` does fail on this machine, and its message is good: it names software

**What the outside view missed — and could not have caught.**

*The white box.* Over ssh, `play` returns `status: done, detail: "opened index.html in the
desktop Browser"`. That is a pass. At the desk it is a HUD floating over a blank white rectangle,
and it is the single worst thing in the app. The whole point of Arcade is that a sentence of
JSON becomes something you can play; `play` is the last step of that promise and it silently
does not happen. The Browser has no WebGL at all — a fact about the machine that no app-level
`describe` mentions anywhere.

*The second face of #97.* I filed `frame_budget` because that is the gate I hit from outside,
driving the stock games. A normal, mildly ambitious game never reaches that gate — it dies at
`bot_reaches_win` first, with a message about **your design**. I only learned that by believing
the message, softening my game like a person would, and getting a **different number on an
identical build**. #97 is half an issue.

*The Blender splash.* From ssh, `describe blender` answers correctly and drives the real scene.
I would have closed that as working. In front of you, the person is looking at Blender's
onboarding dialog while the OS silently rearranges the scene behind it.

*`M10 4V16 M4 10H16`.* Pure pixels. There is no describe field for a button label.

*The window-list mismatch.* You only try to minimise a window when you can see that something is
in the way. From ssh there is nothing in the way.

*"All caught up" over two live toasts.* A disagreement between the store and the screen is, by
construction, invisible to anything that reads the store.

*The false "Yantrik" notification.* You would have to be looking at the person's screen at the
moment a mind posted to it.

**What reads differently with the window in front of you.**

*A failing gate.* As JSON, `failed at frame_budget: 306.8 ms under software rendering` is a
fact, neutral, obviously about the machine. At the desk it arrives after eighty-five to a
hundred and fifty seconds of a progress line that says only "running", in a notice bar that
begins with **your game's name** and the words **"failed verification"**. The clause about
software rendering is the twelfth word. *(Impression, but a strong one: I know this code, I
wrote the gate today, and I still felt the sentence as a verdict on Bramble.)*

*Studio's panel.* From outside it is one boolean, `prompt_leaves_this_machine: true`. At the
desk it is six lines that rewrite themselves in front of you, plus a warning bar, plus a changed
subtitle. It is substantially better than its JSON, and it is the only place on this machine
where a grade change is *explained* rather than just reported.

*The approval card.* A refusal over MCP is one good sentence. The card is a designed object —
the caller's self-declared name held at arm's length beside a kernel-verified one, every
argument listed, a third "for this session" option, a countdown. It is much better in person.
Its failure is also only visible in person: the purpose is cut off mid-word at the exact clause
that would tell you why to care, and the card for turning the hosted service **off** wears the
same warning as the one for turning it **on**.

*Delegation.* This is the thing I would not have believed from a transcript. A mind's work over
ssh is a list of tool calls. At the desk it is **Studio popping to the front, and then a text
editor opening on a JSON file**, because the mind reaches for the same apps you do. You do not
read what it did; you watch it. Nothing in the log conveys that, and it is the best argument
this machine makes for itself.

*What is still the same as this morning.* The taskbar highlight lies (Calendar then, Arcade now).
Email contradicts itself inside one `describe`. Shell screens render under app windows — but now
there is a way out, and that one fix changed the whole feel of the desktop.

## Findings worth an issue

Titles in the repo's voice. **Do not file these — Pranab will.**

1. **Playing a game in the Browser shows a white box, and Arcade reports it as done.**
   The desktop Chromium has no WebGL context at all — `getContext('webgl2'|'webgl'|
   'experimental-webgl')` all return null — so no built game can ever draw. `play` answers
   `status: done · "opened index.html in the desktop Browser"`. The HUD renders, the arena does
   not. Either `play` should check for a context and say what is wrong, or it should fall back
   to the headless screenshot it can already make. *Evidence:* `07-arcade-play.png`,
   `08-webgl-null.png`, `28-arcade-headless.png` (the same game, rendered headless). **Size: medium.**

2. **`verify` tells you your game is bad when it means the machine has no GPU.**
   `bot_reaches_win` failed the same spec and the same build twice with different numbers —
   "collected 5 of 14", then "collected 8 of 14". A trivial game cleared it and failed at
   `frame_budget`, whose message does name software rendering. The win bot is frame-rate bound,
   so on this machine it is a second, unlabelled GPU check. Either budget the bot in simulated
   frames instead of wall clock, or give its failure the same "under software rendering" clause
   `frame_budget` has. *Evidence:* jobs 1, 2, 3 in `describe arcade`. **Size: small.**

3. **There is no way to change a saved game without an approval card.**
   `new_game` refuses an existing title (correctly, and clearly). `delete` is graded `sensitive`,
   so every iteration of a game design needs a click. Game design is iteration. Add
   `update_game`/`update_character`, or a `replace` flag on `new_game`. *Evidence:*
   `refused: A game called "Bramble's Long Dusk" already exists; delete it first or pick another
   title.` **Size: small.**

4. **The window list the shell publishes cannot be used with the window actions beside it.**
   `describe shell` → `{"app": "blender", "title": "Blender"}`. `minimise_window title="Blender"`
   → *"the compositor matched no window: `wlrctl toplevel minimize title:Blender` exited 1"*.
   The real toplevel is `(Unsaved) - Blender 4.3.2`, and passing that is rejected by the shell's
   own validator. No value works for that window. `wlrctl … app_id:Blender` exits 0. The shell
   should match on app_id, or publish the real title, or fall back from `title:` to `app_id:`.
   Also: a phantom "Browser" window with no toplevel behind it, shown twice in the taskbar.
   *Evidence:* `wlrctl toplevel list`; both refusals quoted above. **Size: medium.**

5. **A notification can claim to be from the OS, and the store keeps no record of who sent it.**
   A mind posted, as `app: "Yantrik"`: *"Studio finished making three pictures from the sentence
   you gave it, and the second one took 41 seconds because the fake backend paused"* — one
   picture, 0.05 s, no pause — with a raw `--title …` fragment as the body. The stored record has
   `id, app, title, body, urgency, created_at, read, dismissed, actions, source, revision` and
   nothing about the caller. The approval card already does this properly ("says the caller" vs
   "verified by this machine"); `notify` should carry the same two facts.
   *Evidence:* `24-notifications.png`; `notifications.json` id 134; the sidecar `Pictures/Studio/2026-09-22/194349-18714.json` (`"seconds": 0.05`).
   **Size: medium.**

6. **"All caught up" with two notifications still on the screen.**
   `dismiss_all` empties the centre and leaves the toasts rendered. Critical toasts stay until
   dismissed, by design, but dismissing is a click on the toast's own ×, and no published action
   does it. Toasts should follow the store, and `dismiss`/`dismiss_all` should clear them.
   *Evidence:* `25-after-dismissall.png`. **Size: medium.**

7. **One `sensitive` action, two doors, and only one of them asks.**

## Living here

The fix that changed the most is the smallest one. This morning I wrote that I could not see my
own desktop: five windows open, every shell screen rendering underneath them, and no action to
close, minimise or raise anything. Tonight `minimise_window`, `focus_window`, `show_app` and
`close_window` all exist and all work, and the machine stops feeling like a surface you talk to
through a letterbox. The screens still render underneath — that is not fixed — but there is a
way out, and a way out is most of it.

The second best thing is watching a mind work. Hermes took 35 seconds, and what I saw was
Studio coming to the front and then a text editor opening on a JSON file. It used my apps. I did
not have to trust a summary; I watched. Nothing in a log does that.

What I reached for that was not there: a way to change a game I had just made without an
approval card; a "why" beside `bot_reaches_win`; anything at all telling me the Browser has no
WebGL, at any of the four moments it mattered; a way to read the rest of a truncated approval
card, a truncated mind reply, or a truncated toast; a way to dismiss a toast; a Blender action
that touches Blender's own window; and the three apps I merged today on the desktop's workspace
row, where a new person would look for them.

What surprised me: how much of the honesty is real and how little of it is at the right moment.
This machine will tell you that Workbench draws flat colour — in a refusal about `samples`. It
will tell you it is software-rendering — in the twelfth word of the gate *after* the one that
failed. It will tell you where your prompt goes — beautifully, in a panel, at the exact right
time. The gap between Studio's panel and Arcade's white box is the gap between knowing a thing
and saying it when the person needs it.

What I would keep, precisely: the `set_render` refusal, twenty words that taught me the render
engine. The ceiling refusal that named `tool_permission` in `settings.yaml` rather than just
saying no. The mode-chip panel's four honest lines and the sentence *"This machine never allows a
caller past sensitive, whatever the mode says."* Studio's `set_backend` description, which

provenance. `minimise_window`'s answer telling me where the window went and how to get it back.
Hermes writing *"Backend is already `fake` — I won't call `set_backend` at all."*

And the thing I will remember: I wrote the frame-budget gate this morning, from outside, and I
